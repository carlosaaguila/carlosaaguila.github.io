Rather than passively observing the epileptic network, electrical stimulation lets us actively probe it — revealing connectivity, excitability, and vulnerability that resting-state recordings alone cannot capture.

**Stimulation and spike rates:** Low-frequency stimulation (1 Hz LFS) acutely modulates interictal spike rates across the epileptogenic network, but effects are heterogeneous — mesial temporal structures are most vulnerable. This work directly informs how closed-loop devices should be programmed.

**Cortico-cortical evoked potentials (CCEPs):** I contribute to a project modeling CCEP amplitude and morphology as a function of distance, brain state (sleep vs. wake), and epileptogenicity. CCEPs provide a direct measure of effective connectivity between stimulated and recorded regions.

**Methods:** Signal processing pipelines for evoked response extraction, mixed-effects models, Python / MATLAB.

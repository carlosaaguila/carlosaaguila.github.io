BE 5210: Brain Computer Interfaces

Graduate course covering the neuroscience, engineering, and clinical translation of brain-computer interfaces. Topics include neural signal acquisition, signal processing, decoding algorithms, closed-loop systems, and clinical applications in motor restoration, speech, and epilepsy.

Instructor: Graduate Teaching Fellow (Primary instructor — Prof. Brian Litt, MD)

Term: Spring

Location: University of Pennsylvania, Department of Bioengineering

Time: See course website

Course Overview

BE 5210 (formerly numbered BE 521) covers the full stack of brain-computer interface technology — from the biophysics of neural signal generation to clinical deployment of BCI devices. The course is designed for bioengineering graduate students and provides both theoretical foundations and practical exposure to modern BCI research.

I have served as Graduate Teaching Fellow for BE 5210 since January 2023 — three consecutive years — supporting lecture delivery, leading discussion sections, designing problem sets, and mentoring students through final projects.

Course website: be5210.com

Topics Covered

  • Neural signal acquisition and electrode technology
  • Signal processing and feature extraction
  • Motor and speech BCIs
  • Closed-loop neuromodulation and epilepsy applications
  • Wearable and minimally invasive devices
  • Regulatory pathways and ethics

My Role

Graduate Teaching Fellow under Prof. Brian Litt, MD. Responsibilities include lecture support, office hours, assignment design, and project mentoring. Taught continuously 2023–2025.

Schedule

Week Date Topic Materials
1 Introduction to BCIs

History, taxonomy, and clinical landscape of brain-computer interfaces.
2 Neural Signal Acquisition

Electrode types (ECoG, SEEG, Utah array, EEG), impedance, signal quality, and recording systems.
3 Signal Processing Fundamentals

Filtering, spectral analysis, artifact rejection, and feature extraction from neural signals.
4 Spike Detection & Sorting

Single-unit and multi-unit recording, spike sorting algorithms, and population coding.
5 Decoding Algorithms

Linear decoders, Kalman filters, population vector methods, and deep learning approaches.
6 Motor BCIs

Intracortical and ECoG-based motor restoration — BrainGate, Stentrode, and commercial systems.
7 Speech BCIs

Decoding intended speech from neural activity — phoneme, word, and sentence-level approaches.
8 Closed-Loop Neuromodulation

Responsive neurostimulation (RNS), DBS, spinal cord stimulation — feedback control architecture.
9 Epilepsy Applications

Seizure detection, prediction, and responsive stimulation. Clinical trial landscape.
10 Non-Invasive BCIs

EEG-based BCIs, P300 spellers, SSVEP, and consumer-grade systems.
11 Wearable & Sub-Scalp Devices

Next-generation minimally invasive recording platforms and ambulatory monitoring.
12 Regulatory & Ethical Considerations

FDA pathways for neurotechnology, informed consent, neural data privacy, and cognitive liberty.
13 Clinical Translation

From bench to bedside — what it takes to go from prototype to approved device.
14 Student Presentations

Final project presentations.
